# Craft

The look comes from constraint: one camera, one material system, a few tones, finished parts and few words. Each rule below records the defect that taught it, so it can be dropped if the reason stops holding.

## Form

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Build the concept's own elements as finished parts: housings with seams, recessed screens, rack units and vents, turned round bodies | Plain blocks read as a diagram; the first retries draft used cubes for client, server and ledger, and nothing said what each one was |
| One part per real element, counted true: one coin per charge, one slot per capacity unit | The picture is evidence; the $40 stack of two coins is the explanation |
| Detail belongs to a part and moves with it (`surface`) | Decoration overlaid on the scene drifted off moving parts |
| A part rests on something: plinth, plate or tray | Floating solids read as an error |
| Distinct functions get distinct silhouettes | Identical boxes for different roles forced the reader to rely on labels |

## Colour and tone

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Neutral by default; colour only for meaning (hit, miss, lost, duplicate, stored, processing), one meaning per tone, stated in the legend | Saturated primaries everywhere made nothing stand out |
| Exactly one `lit` thing per step | Several lit parts split attention |
| The ink of face labels is chosen by measured contrast | White text failed 4.5:1 on light tops, and a fixed rule failed some tone in some theme |
| Never use a background gradient on the stage | A faint radial gradient banded into visible rings on 8-bit displays |

## Words

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Title of 8 words at most, one action of 12 words at most per step, part names of 1–3 words | Paragraphs beside the figure were read instead of the figure |
| Names in a row in front of the parts, each with a leader to its part | Names scattered on the plates collided with parts and with each other |
| A label never lies on a part other than its target (checked) | "Idempotency key" sat across the terminal's base |
| A face label that does not fit is not drawn; optional labels drop where they would collide | Screen text spilled off a phone-sized terminal; slot numbers landed on the guide rail |
| Lane and system titles go beside their plate, near the outcome | A title between two plates was read as naming the wrong one |

## Motion

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Exits, then moves, then entries | A slot briefly held two things |
| A reorder hops over its neighbours (`arc`) | A hit passed through the entries shifting the other way |
| An entity that leaves goes somewhere meaningful (`leaving`) | An eviction that just faded did not say "pushed off the cold end" |
| Messages ride a visible cable in front of the actors | Messages at mid-depth were hidden behind the server |
| The stage keeps one size at every step (checked) | A strip that grew by 6 px made the camera fit history-dependent |

## Composition

- The figure dominates one desktop viewport (1440×900 and 1920×1080). Its solids span at least 42% of the stage width.
- Fit the camera over every step, so nothing jumps.
- Parallel systems (twin worlds) go on separate plates along depth, front plate nearest, with the same layout on each.
- Wide scenes shrink on phones rather than re-arranging. Expect hidden face labels there; nothing essential may live only on a face label.

## What the checks cannot judge

Whether the representation is the right one, whether the composition is beautiful, and whether the steps teach. Look at the step and motion contact sheets and at 4× close-ups of every junction, and compare against the reference examples.
